Public vs. Private Healthcare in Spain: What Expats Need to Know

By Alisa Zinko · May 2026 · 9 min read

Spain has one of the best healthcare systems in Europe. The question is not whether it is good — it is how to access it, and whether private insurance is worth adding on top. This guide gives you the full picture.

Healthcare is one of the first things people ask about when planning a move to Spain. The Spanish system can be excellent, but it works very differently from the NHS or US insurance model, and getting registered correctly matters from day one.

This guide covers how the public system works, who can access it, how to register, what private insurance costs, and how to decide what setup makes sense for your situation.

How Spain's Healthcare System Works

Spain has two parallel healthcare systems: the public Sistema Nacional de Salud (SNS) and a large private sector. Most expats end up using both.

The SNS is funded through taxes and covers residents at no direct cost for most treatments: GP visits, specialist referrals, surgery, hospital stays, emergency care, and heavily subsidised prescriptions. It is managed at the regional level, which means quality and waiting times vary depending on where you live.

The private sector operates separately. You pay a monthly premium for a policy that gives you access to a network of private clinics and hospitals, usually with much shorter waiting times, English-speaking doctors in major cities, and no referral required to see a specialist.

Who Can Access Spain's Public Healthcare?

Access to the SNS depends on your legal status in Spain, not your nationality.

EU citizens

EU citizens can use their European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) for temporary stays. If you are moving permanently, the S1 form (issued by your home country's social security system) entitles you to full SNS access immediately. This is the most straightforward route for EU nationals relocating to Spain.

Non-EU residents with legal residency

Once you have a valid residency permit (TIE) and are registered on the Padrón municipal, you can register with your local health centre and access the full SNS. You do not need to be working or paying into social security, though some regions apply their own rules for non-contributing residents.

Non-Lucrative Visa holders

During the visa application process, private health insurance is mandatory — it is a condition of the visa. Once you arrive in Spain, register on the Padrón, and obtain your TIE, you can register with the public system. Many NLV holders keep their private insurance alongside public access, at least for the first year or two.

Tourists and short-stay visitors

Only emergency treatment is available. Spain will stabilise any emergency, but ongoing or elective care requires either private insurance or payment out of pocket.

How to Register for Public Healthcare in Spain

The process is straightforward once you have your documentation in order. Here is what you need and what to do.

What you need

  • Valid passport or national ID
  • NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero)
  • TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero — your residency card)
  • Padrón certificate (empadronamiento) from your local town hall
  • Proof of address in Spain (rental contract or utility bill)

Step by step

  1. Register on the Padrón at your local Ayuntamiento (town hall). This is the foundation — without it, you cannot register for healthcare.
  2. Go to your nearest centro de salud (health centre). Bring originals and copies of all documents above.
  3. Request registration (inscripción en el sistema sanitario) and ask for your health card. Depending on the region, this is called a SIP card (Valencia), TSI (Tarjeta Sanitaria Individual), or CIP.
  4. You will be assigned a GP (médico de cabecera). All specialist referrals go through your GP first.

Processing is usually immediate or takes a few days. In some regions, particularly the Valencian Community, you receive a temporary paper and the physical card arrives by post within a few weeks.

Optical / vision Not covered

The main gaps in the public system are dental care, optical, physiotherapy, and mental health. These are where private insurance or out-of-pocket payments typically come in, even for people who rely primarily on the public system.

Private Health Insurance in Spain: Options and Costs

Spain has a well-developed private healthcare market with competitive prices compared to the UK and US. The most commonly used providers among expats are Sanitas, Adeslas, Asisa, DKV, and Cigna International.

What private insurance typically covers

  • GP and specialist visits without GP referral
  • Diagnostic tests and imaging (MRI, blood work, X-rays)
  • Surgery and hospitalisation in private facilities
  • Dental care (depending on the policy)
  • Mental health sessions
  • Physiotherapy
  • English-speaking doctors in major cities (check your insurer's network)

These are indicative figures. Premiums vary by insurer, region, pre-existing conditions, and the level of cover selected. Policies with dental and physiotherapy included cost more. Cigna and AXA offer international policies for those who travel frequently or split time between countries.

Important: policies for the Non-Lucrative Visa

If you need insurance to satisfy the NLV visa requirement, the policy must have no co-pays and full coverage in Spain with no limit on reimbursement. Not all expat policies meet this standard. Always check with your Spanish consulate before purchasing. Sanitas Xpat and Cigna Global are commonly accepted; many travel or basic expat policies are not.

The most common setup among long-term expats

Most settled expats in Spain use a combination: they register with the public system for serious conditions, surgery, and emergencies, and keep a private policy (or pay out of pocket) for faster specialist access, dental, and English-speaking consultations. This is often the most cost-effective and practical approach.

Younger expats in good health often drop private insurance once they are registered with the SNS. Older expats and families typically keep it.

Regional Differences That Matter

Because healthcare in Spain is managed regionally, your experience will vary depending on where you live. A few things to know:

  • Catalonia, Madrid, and the Basque Country have the best-resourced public health systems and shorter waiting times.
  • The Valencian Community (Valencia, Alicante, Castellon) uses the SIP card system. Registration is generally efficient and widely documented for expats.
  • Rural areas across all regions have fewer specialist facilities and longer travel times to hospitals. Private insurance is more useful in these areas.
  • The Canary Islands and Balearics have fully functional SNS systems, though island logistics can mean longer waits for certain specialist procedures.

Prescriptions in Spain

Prescriptions through the public system are subsidised. The amount you pay depends on your income and employment status:

  • Working residents pay between 10% and 60% of prescription cost, depending on salary
  • Pensioners and low-income residents pay a reduced rate, often 10% with a monthly cap
  • Children and certain chronic conditions are exempt from co-payments

Private prescriptions (written by a private doctor) are not subsidised and are paid in full at the pharmacy. For common medications, this is often still much cheaper than equivalent costs in the UK or US.

Emergency Care in Spain

Spain's emergency services are excellent and available to everyone regardless of residency or insurance status. Emergency treatment will never be refused. The emergency number in Spain is 112 (police, fire, ambulance). Hospital emergency departments are called Urgencias.

Non-residents who use emergency services may receive a bill afterward if they do not have an EHIC or valid insurance. Residents with SNS registration are not charged for emergency care.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use my UK NHS entitlement in Spain?

Not directly. Post-Brexit, UK citizens no longer have automatic access to Spanish public healthcare. You need either an S1 form (if you receive a UK state pension or are posted to Spain by a UK employer), a valid residency permit and Padrón registration, or private insurance.

Can I keep my home country insurance and use it in Spain?

Some international health insurance policies provide coverage in Spain. Check whether Spain is included, whether it meets Spanish consulate requirements if you need it for a visa, and whether it covers you at Spanish public or private facilities.

What happens if I need hospital treatment before I am registered?

Emergency treatment is always available. For non-emergency hospital care before you have SNS registration, you will need private insurance or to pay out of pocket. This is one reason why registering on the Padrón and with your health centre should be among your first priorities after arriving.

Does Spain have good mental health support?

The public system has limited mental health capacity: GP referral to a psychiatrist is possible, but waiting times are long and English-language therapists are rare through the public route. Private insurance typically includes mental health cover, and there is a growing network of English-speaking therapists and psychologists available privately in major cities.
